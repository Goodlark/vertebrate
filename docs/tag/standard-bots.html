Humanoid Robots Hit a Wall — Literally and Figuratively

A string of stories underscores how far humanoid robots still have to go before matching hype with performance. CNBC reports that Chinese manufacturers, despite dominating the field, keep bumping against the plain fact that humans remain more efficient and adaptable workers. That gap turned literal when Unitree's speedy new 'Superman' robot, clocked at Usain Bolt-level speeds during trials for Beijing's 2026 World Humanoid Robot Games, ran straight into a wall in a clip that went viral. Meanwhile, a federal ban on foreign-made humanoid robots is reshaping the US market: RoboStore, once the top domestic distributor of Chinese models, is launching its own manufacturing arm, Robo Inc., to build robots stateside. Industry voice Jeff Burnstein, of the Association for Advancing Automation, frames the moment starkly — humanoid robots must now prove they can solve real business problems, not just impress on camera.
